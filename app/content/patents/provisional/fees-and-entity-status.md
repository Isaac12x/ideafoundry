USPTO fees depend on filing type and on entity size. Always read the current fee schedule; amounts change.

Entity sizes:
- Large (undiscounted): the default.
- Small: fewer than 500 employees including affiliates, and no license or assignment obligation to a larger entity. Also nonprofits and universities.
- Micro: qualifies as small, and also: named on no more than four earlier non-provisional applications (certain applications excluded), income below a limit based on median household income, and no assignment or license to an entity above that limit. A university-based route also exists. A certification is required.

Rules:
- Status applies fee by fee, at the time of payment.
- Licensing or assigning to a large entity ends the discount; tell the USPTO before the next fee.
- Claiming status wrongly in good faith can be corrected by paying the difference. Fraud can make a patent unenforceable.
- Many fees appear at several stages: filing, search, examination, excess claims, issue, and maintenance. Provisionals have a single filing fee.
- Foreign and PCT filings have their own fees.

Example: a solo inventor who already assigned an earlier invention to a large company should check micro-entity rules before assuming the discount.
