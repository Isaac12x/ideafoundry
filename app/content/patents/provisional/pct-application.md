Two different clocks are often merged:

Paris Convention priority: file a corresponding application in another member country within 12 months of your first filing (including a US provisional; 6 months for designs) and get that first date as the priority date there.

PCT: one international application, filed with a receiving office (the USPTO works for US residents). It does not give an "international patent." It:
- Can claim Paris priority if filed within 12 months of the first filing.
- Gets an international search and a written opinion on patentability.
- Is published at 18 months from the priority date.
- Must enter the national or regional phase in each country you want, typically at 30 months from the earliest priority date (some offices 31). This is where examination and grant happen.

Why use it: delays costly country-by-country decisions while keeping the priority date.

Limits: it does not fix prior disclosure. Foreign absolute novelty still applies.

US applicants: a foreign filing license is needed before filing abroad within a set period after a US filing; the filing receipt usually grants it. Foreign filing without one can jeopardize the US patent.

Example: provisional filed 1 Mar 2026; PCT filed by 1 Mar 2027 claiming it; national phases around Sep 2028.
