In US law (35 USC 102(b)(1)), a disclosure made one year or less before the effective filing date is not prior art against the claim if:
- (A) it was made by the inventor or joint inventor (or by someone who obtained the subject matter from them), or
- (B) an intervening third-party disclosure came after the inventor's own public disclosure of that same subject matter.

Details that catch people:
- It protects only the inventor's own disclosures and those derived from them, not independent work by others made earlier.
- It covers only what you disclosed. Variations you did not publish may not be protected.
- Sales, offers for sale and public use count as disclosures; secret commercial sales can too.
- The year runs to the effective filing date. A supporting provisional filed inside the year can stop the clock.
- It is a rescue, not a strategy. Proving the exception can be costly, and third parties can still file independently.
- It is a US rule. Most other jurisdictions have no grace period or a much narrower one; see foreign absolute novelty.

Example: you post a preprint on 1 Mar 2026. A US application filed by 1 Mar 2027 can exclude the preprint as prior art. The same preprint may defeat novelty in Europe.
