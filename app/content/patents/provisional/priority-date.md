"Priority date" is the practical name for the date that decides what counts as prior art against you. In US law (since 2013, first-inventor-to-file) the term is the effective filing date, set claim by claim.

A claim's effective filing date is the earlier of:
- the application's actual filing date, or
- the filing date of an earlier application it validly claims benefit of (provisional, parent, or a foreign application under Paris priority).

The earlier date only works if that earlier application supports the claim: written description and enablement for that claim. Otherwise the claim gets the later date.

Why it matters:
- Anything public before the effective filing date, beyond the grace period, can be prior art.
- Earliest filer wins against independent inventors; the date you invented no longer matters.
- Different claims in one application can have different dates.

Terminology:
- Domestic "benefit" (provisional, continuation) versus foreign "priority" (Paris Convention, 12 months). Both are claimed in the data sheet.
- Patent term is counted from the non-provisional filing date, not the priority date.

Example: provisional filed 1 June describes feature A only; non-provisional filed 1 May next year adds feature B. A-claims date from 1 June; B-claims date from 1 May.
