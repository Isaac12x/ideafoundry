Absolute novelty: an invention is not new if it was made available to the public anywhere in the world, in any form, before the filing (or valid priority) date. The European Patent Convention (Art. 54) works this way.

Contrast with the US:
- The US allows a one-year grace period for the inventor's own disclosures.
- The EPO has no general grace period, only narrow exceptions (e.g., evident abuse, certain official exhibitions within six months).
- Other countries vary: some have limited grace periods with formalities and shorter or narrower scope; some, like China, allow only specific situations. Check each target country before disclosing.

Consequences:
- Your own talk, paper, demo, product launch, or public repository can bar foreign patents if it precedes the filing or priority date.
- Disclosure under confidentiality may be safe, but proof matters.
- A first filing (even a US provisional) before disclosure keeps foreign options open, because other countries recognize it as the priority date for 12 months under the Paris Convention.

Working rule: if foreign rights matter at all, file before any public disclosure.

Example: a founder presents at a conference, then files a US application within six months. The US application can rely on the grace period, but a European application is likely novelty-barred.
