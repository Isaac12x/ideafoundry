A provisional application (35 USC 111(b)) is a low-formality US filing that locks in a filing date. It is never examined and never becomes a patent. It goes abandoned after 12 months by operation of law.

What it does:
- Gives a filing date that a later non-provisional can claim (35 USC 119(e)), but only for what the provisional actually disclosed.
- Lets you say "patent pending".
- Needs no claims, oath or declaration, and no formal drawings.
- Is not published unless a later application claiming it is published.

What it does not do: grant any enforceable right, or start the 20-year term. The term runs from the non-provisional filing date, so a provisional effectively delays the end of protection.

Deadlines:
- File the non-provisional (or a PCT/foreign application) within 12 months. If that date is a weekend or federal holiday, the next business day counts.
- If missed unintentionally, a restoration petition can save a US benefit claim when the non-provisional is filed within 14 months. This is a rescue, not a plan, and foreign priority rights are governed by other rules.

The value of a provisional equals the quality of its disclosure. Thin "idea lists" give no priority for later claims.

Example: file on 1 March; the later application is due by 1 March next year, restorable only to 1 May, and only for the US benefit claim.
