Two views of the same claims.

Design-around (the competitor's view): infringement needs every element of a claim (literally, or by equivalents). Remove or replace one element and you avoid that claim. Check every independent claim and every family member.

Licensing (your view): claims you can license have clear coverage of a product or standard. Subsets can be licensed separately.

Drafting checklist:
1. Write a mock product that avoids claim 1; if easy, claim 1 is too narrow.
2. Add independent claims or family members for the alternatives you found.
3. Prefer claims covering what an infringer does (methods run, systems sold), over steps hidden inside one party's server.
4. Chart real products against claims before licensing talks.
5. Record ownership and assignments cleanly.

Notes: freedom to operate (whether you infringe others) is different from patentability; a patent gives a right to exclude, not a right to practice. Seek counsel before licensing or asserting.
