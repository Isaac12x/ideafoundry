A CIP repeats the parent's disclosure and adds new matter, then claims both. It must be filed while the parent is pending and share at least one inventor.

Date rule, claim by claim:
- Claim fully supported by the parent: gets the parent's date.
- Claim needing the new matter: gets only the CIP's filing date, so newer prior art (including your own publications in between) can be used against it.

Term: measured from the earliest non-provisional date claimed, even though some claims are only entitled to later dates.

When to use: an improvement was invented after the parent was filed (e.g. a cloud ML scoring layer added to a local-lookup parent) and you want one combined spec.

Alternatives: often cleaner to file the improvement as a separate new application (its own date and term, no entanglement), and keep continuations for subject matter already disclosed. A CIP mixes dates, which complicates validity analysis.

Check each claim's support against the parent before relying on the earlier date (see claim-to-spec-mapping).
