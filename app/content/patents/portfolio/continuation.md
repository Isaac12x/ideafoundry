A continuation is a new application with the same disclosure as an earlier ("parent") application but new claims. It adds no new matter and takes the parent's filing date for what the parent supports (35 USC 120).

Requirements:
- Filed while the parent is still pending (copendency), i.e. before it issues or is abandoned.
- Same inventor named in at least one place (overlapping inventorship).
- Benefit claim made in the application data sheet.

Why file one:
- Pursue broader or different claims than the parent allowed.
- Cover a competitor's later product with claims the original spec already supports.
- Keep a family "open" (see pending-continuation-strategy).

Limits (common misconceptions):
- Term does not extend. All family members expire 20 years from the earliest non-provisional filing date claimed (see patent-term).
- Claims too close to the parent can draw an obviousness-type double patenting rejection, curable by a terminal disclaimer.
- Endless refiling to delay can trigger prosecution laches.

Example: parent claims the QR verification method; a continuation, filed before the parent issues, claims the system and the computer-readable medium.
