Idea: before a family member issues, file a continuation so something stays pending. You can later draft claims aimed at competitor products the original spec already supports.

Checklist:
1. File the continuation before the parent issues or is abandoned (copendency).
2. Benefit claim in the application data sheet.
3. Claims must be supported by the original spec (no new matter).
4. Draft new claims for real market targets, not placeholders.
5. Watch the 20-year clock; late continuations have short remaining life.
6. Plan for terminal disclaimers (common ownership).
7. Budget for filing, examination and maintenance fees across the family (see the USPTO fee schedule).

Limits:
- Delay does not extend term.
- Prosecution laches can make a patent unenforceable if filing patterns are unreasonable and prejudicial (see prosecution-laches).
- Claims must differ patentably or need a terminal disclaimer.
- Don't copy a competitor product into claims unless the original spec truly supports it.

_Educational, not legal advice._
