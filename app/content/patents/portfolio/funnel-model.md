Picture the portfolio as a funnel: broad claims at the top, increasingly specific commercial-implementation claims at the bottom.

How it plays out:
1. Parent: broad but concrete claims (still tied to a technical improvement).
2. If the examiner narrows scope, take the narrower allowance to get a patent issued.
3. File a continuation to keep pursuing the broader scope with new arguments or evidence.
4. Lower members claim the exact product implementation.

Why: broad claims give leverage and wide coverage; narrow ones are more likely allowed and harder to invalidate.

Cautions:
- "As abstract as possible" is wrong for software: broad is fine, abstract is not (see alice-two-step).
- Narrowing amendments made for patentability presume surrender of equivalents (see festo-estoppel).
- Keep the specification supporting every level of the funnel, otherwise broad continuation claims fail written description.
- Repeated tries must stay reasonable (see prosecution-laches).

_Educational, not legal advice._
