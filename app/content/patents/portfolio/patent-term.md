Utility patents generally expire 20 years from the earliest effective non-provisional filing date (35 USC 154(a)(2)). Design patents run on a different term (from grant).

What counts:
- Continuation, divisional, CIP: term runs from the parent's non-provisional date they claim benefit to, not their own filing date.
- Provisional: not counted. Filing one does not start the 20 years.
- PCT: the international filing date.

Adjustments:
- Patent term adjustment can add days for certain USPTO delays.
- A terminal disclaimer (from double patenting) cuts the later patent's term to the earlier patent's.
- Patent term extension exists for some regulated products.

Keeping it alive: maintenance fees are due at set intervals after grant (about 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years); missing them ends the patent. See the USPTO pages for current amounts and grace periods.

Example: parent filed in year 0, continuation granted in year 6 claiming its benefit: both expire at year 20 (before adjustments).
