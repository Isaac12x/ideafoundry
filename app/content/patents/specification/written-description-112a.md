35 USC 112(a) has separate requirements: written description, enablement, and best mode. **Written description** asks whether the original application shows the inventor was **in possession** of the claimed invention at filing (Ariad v. Eli Lilly, Fed. Cir. 2010, en banc). Ariad is the case that confirmed this is a requirement separate from enablement; the source wrongly cites it for enablement.

What it demands:
- Every claim limitation should be found, expressly or inherently, in the filed specification.
- Broad genus claims need adequate representative species or structural features, a major issue in chemistry and biology.
- For computer-implemented functions, the specification must disclose an algorithm or enough steps to show possession.
- Amendments and later claims cannot add matter beyond what was disclosed (see New matter).

Example: a claim reciting "a local database" is supported if the specification describes a local database. A claim to "any storage" with only a disk disclosed may lack support for the genus.

Tip: build a mapping table from each claim limitation to specification paragraphs and figures before filing.
