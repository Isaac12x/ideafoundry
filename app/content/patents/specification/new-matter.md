New matter is subject matter added to an application after filing that was not in the original disclosure. 35 USC 132 prohibits adding it to a pending application, and 112(a) invalidates claims that rely on it.

Why it matters:
- Examiners object to amendments to the specification or drawings that add new matter, and reject claims under 112(a) if they lack original support.
- You cannot cure missing support by adding text later; the fix is a continuation-in-part (see CIP), which gets a later date for the added material.
- Provisional benefit: only matter supported in the provisional gets its date.

Allowed: clarifications and correcting obvious errors supported by the original text, and material incorporated by reference if properly identified at filing.

Example: adding a "blockchain" verification embodiment after filing when the original text only described a local database is new matter.

Tip: include broad embodiments, alternatives, and technical details at filing; it is cheaper than a later CIP. Use incorporation by reference carefully as a safeguard, not a substitute.
