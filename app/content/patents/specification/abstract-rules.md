The abstract is a concise summary that lets readers and searchers quickly grasp what the disclosure is about. Under 37 CFR 1.72(b):

- On its own sheet, headed "Abstract" or "Abstract of the Disclosure".
- 150 words or fewer.
- One paragraph, narrative form; avoid legal phraseology such as "means" or "said".
- No claim references or drawing figure numbers required; do not describe merits or speculative applications.
- Should state the technical disclosure, including what is new.

It has no effect on claim scope and cannot be used to interpret claims narrowly in the usual way, but it is searchable and published, so accuracy matters. The USPTO may object to an abstract that is too long or too vague.

Example: "A method verifies a link by decoding an encoded identifier from an image, comparing a hostname to a stored list, and blocking navigation when no match is found."

Tip: write the abstract last and align it to the independent claim's wording.
