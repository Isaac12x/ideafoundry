The "person having ordinary skill in the art" (PHOSITA or POSITA) is a hypothetical reader used across patent law: for obviousness (35 USC 103), enablement and written description (112(a)), and claim interpretation.

Factors for the level of skill (MPEP 2141.03): educational level, types of problems met, prior art solutions, speed of innovation, sophistication of the technology, and workers' education.

The perspective is time-specific: as of the effective filing date. The person is not the inventor and is not a genius; they know the art, can follow instructions, and use common sense (KSR).

How it affects drafting: the specification need not describe what a skilled person already knows (such as standard libraries or protocols), but it must supply the details that are not known, in particular the inventive step. If you rely on "known techniques", name them or cite sources.

Example: a software invention can omit how a standard QR decoder works but must explain the new verification logic.

Tip: define the field in the background so the intended reader (a mobile security engineer, say) is clear.
