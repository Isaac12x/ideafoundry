The Background gives the field and the problem, without admitting that your invention is old. Under 37 CFR 1.77(b)(6), it may include the field and a description of related art, but its content can later be used against you.

Guidelines:
- State the field plainly ("link verification on mobile devices").
- Describe the problem in general terms; do not blame specific competitors.
- Avoid statements like "it is well known that X", especially about features you may later claim; they can be treated as admissions of prior art.
- Do not criticise the prior art in ways that disclaim scope (courts have limited claims by "the present invention" disparagement).
- Keep it factual and short; the technical solution belongs in the summary and description.

Example: "Mobile users scan codes that encode network addresses. Verifying the destination before opening presents tradeoffs among latency, privacy, and accuracy."

Tip: write it neutrally enough to survive scrutiny but concrete enough to frame the technical problem for the improvement argument.
