35 USC 112(a) still says the specification "shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor" for carrying out the invention. It is a subjective test: did the inventor, at filing, know a preferred way to practice the invention and conceal it?

Post-AIA change: since the America Invents Act (2011), failure to disclose the best mode is **not** a basis for cancelling or invalidating a patent or holding it unenforceable (35 USC 282(b)(3)). Examiners rarely reject on best mode in practice (MPEP 2165), and the requirement remains in the statute mainly as an expectation of candour.

Correction: the sources present best mode as a continuing source of invalidity; that is no longer true for US patents. Other countries have differing rules.

Example: if the inventor's preferred algorithm is a particular signature check, the specification should describe it, not just a generic "verification".

Tip: still disclose preferred embodiments; it helps enablement and written description anyway.
