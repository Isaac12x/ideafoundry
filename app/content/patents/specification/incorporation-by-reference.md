Incorporation by reference (IBR) is a sentence that makes another document part of your specification: "The disclosure of US 12/345,678 is incorporated herein by reference in its entirety."

Rules and limits (37 CFR 1.57; MPEP 608.01(p)):
- Identify the document clearly (number, title, date).
- "Essential material" must be in the specification or in an incorporated US patent or published application, not in a non-patent document or foreign patent.
- Do not incorporate by reference to avoid explaining your own invention.
- An incorporation statement present on the filing date can supply missing material only if it was there at filing.

Correction from sources: such a sentence does not by itself establish a priority claim to a provisional. A benefit claim is made in the application data sheet (37 CFR 1.78). IBR of the provisional is a separate, useful safeguard, and 37 CFR 1.57(b) can allow inadvertently omitted material to be added if the benefit claim was present at filing.

Example: "This application claims the benefit of provisional application No. XX; the provisional is incorporated by reference."

Tip: IBR is a backup; include the key text.
