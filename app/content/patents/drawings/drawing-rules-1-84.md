Drawings are required when needed to understand the invention, and must show every feature recited in the claims (35 USC 113, 37 CFR 1.83). Rule 1.84 governs how they look.

Key points for a utility application:
- Black, sufficiently dark, uniformly thick, well-defined lines. Color or photos need a petition (and are rare).
- Sheet: 21.6 x 27.9 cm (US Letter) or A4 (21.0 x 29.7 cm).
- Minimum margins (1.84(g)): top 2.5 cm, left 2.5 cm, right 1.5 cm, bottom 1.0 cm.
- Text and reference characters at least 0.32 cm (about 1/8 in) tall; the drawing must stay legible when reduced to two-thirds.
- Reference characters: plain Arabic numerals/letters, not in brackets, quotes or circles. Same part = same numeral in every view; never reuse one numeral for two parts.
- Every numeral in the description must appear in the drawings, and the reverse.
- Sheets numbered consecutively "1/3, 2/3..." in the top middle, outside the margin area. Views labeled "FIG. 1", etc.
- Block diagrams and flowcharts need short descriptive legends in the boxes.
- No new drawings after filing: that is new matter.

Provisional applications do not need formal drawings, but a clear sketch still counts as disclosure.

Example: a flowchart box reading "Decode QR code" with numeral 204 beside it, and "204" mentioned in the Detailed Description.

Check the live rule text before filing; exact margins and sheet limits are easy to misquote.
