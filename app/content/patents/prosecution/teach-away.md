A reference teaches away when a person of ordinary skill, reading it, would be discouraged from the path the applicant took, or led in a different direction. It is a recognised rebuttal to an obviousness rejection.

What counts:
- Explicit criticism, discrediting, or warning against the combination or approach.
- A reference whose purpose or principle of operation would be defeated by the proposed modification.

What does not count:
- A mere preference for another option.
- Noting a disadvantage while still disclosing the approach as usable.
- Attorney argument with no support in the reference.

Example (illustrative): Reference B states "pre-launch URL analysis must not be used, because it makes the app unreliable." That can be argued to discourage the claimed pre-launch check. If B only says "analysis can add delay" while offering the technique anyway, it is likely a disadvantage, not a teaching away.

Quote the passage and explain the effect; do not paraphrase loosely. Post-KSR, examiners may still reason that benefits outweigh the downside, so support your argument with the reference's own words and, where available, evidence.
