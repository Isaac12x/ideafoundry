An examiner interview is a conversation (phone, video, or in person) between the applicant or representative and the examiner about a pending application. It can clear up misunderstandings fast.

Good uses:
- Clarify what a reference actually shows or what a claim term means.
- Show a proposed amendment and ask whether it would overcome the rejection.
- Discuss a demonstration of the invention.
- Narrow issues before a response or after a final rejection.

Rules of thumb:
- Request it in advance, with an agenda and any proposed claim language.
- An interview does not replace a written reply: the applicant must still file a statement of the substance in the record, within the reply period.
- Examiners can give a view but are not bound by it until allowance.
- Whatever you say is part of the file history and can be used in later claim interpretation and estoppel.

Example (illustrative): an examiner says "pre-launch" in claim 1 would overcome Smith. You propose wording, then file it formally with the argument.

Check the USPTO interview practice page for current options and procedures.
