An office action is the examiner's written report on your application: which claims are allowed, rejected, or objected to, and why.

Common grounds:
- 101 eligibility; 102 anticipation; 103 obviousness.
- 112 (indefinite, not enabled, no written description).
- Obviousness-type double patenting.
- Objections (form) and restriction requirements.

A first action is usually "non-final", so you may amend and reply. A reply must address every ground raised. The reply period is set in the action; it can often be extended for a fee up to a statutory maximum (see USPTO fee page via MPEP link), and missing it abandons the application unless revived.

Your options: argue the examiner is wrong, amend the claims, or both; request an interview; file a continuation; or, in some cases, accept allowable claims and pursue the rest separately.

Example (illustrative): claim 1 is rejected under 103 over Smith in view of Johnson. You chart the claim against both, spot that neither checks a URL before launching it, and respond with arguments plus a narrowing amendment with specification support.

Everything you say becomes part of the public file history and can limit enforcement later.

_Educational, not legal advice._
