Secondary considerations (objective indicia of non-obviousness) are real-world facts that suggest an invention was not obvious. They are the fourth Graham factor.

Examples:
- Commercial success of the claimed product.
- Long-felt but unsolved need.
- Failure of others.
- Industry praise and licensing.
- Copying by competitors.
- Skepticism of experts, and unexpected results.

Key rule: nexus. The evidence must tie to the features actually claimed, not to marketing, price, or other factors. Success from an unclaimed feature carries little weight.

How to submit: facts in a declaration (sales, timelines, press, attempts by others), with comparison to the claim scope. Evidence must be real and verifiable; do not invent it.

Example (illustrative): sales of a QR scanner rose after launch. That helps only if you can link the rise to the claimed pre-launch check rather than to a price cut or a new brand.

An examiner must consider timely submitted evidence together with the rest of the record.
