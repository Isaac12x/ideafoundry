A restriction requirement is an examiner's demand that you pick one invention to prosecute when the claims cover two or more independent or distinct inventions (35 USC 121, MPEP 800 series). It is not a rejection.

How it works:
1. The examiner groups claims into inventions (e.g. a method group and a hardware group).
2. You elect one group, with or without traverse (arguing the restriction is improper). A traversal must be made to preserve review.
3. Unelected claims are withdrawn, not abandoned, and can be pursued in one or more divisional applications.

Species election works similarly: you pick a species to examine first.

Why it matters: more groups mean more applications and fees; a restriction can split your portfolio. Under 121, divisionals filed due to a restriction get protection from double patenting only if they stay consonant.

Example (illustrative): claims cover a QR scanning method and a separate hardware cache. The examiner restricts; you elect the method, then file a divisional for the cache.

Note the filing deadline: file the divisional while the parent is pending to keep priority.
