Each individual substantively involved in an application (inventors, attorneys, others) has a duty of candor and good faith: disclose information known to be material to patentability (37 CFR 1.56). The usual way is an Information Disclosure Statement (IDS) listing references.

Key points:
- There is no general duty to search, but known material references must be disclosed.
- Material means information that would matter to an examiner's patentability decision, such as a reference that anticipates or is not cumulative of those already in the record.
- Timing matters: an IDS filed early (before the first action on the merits) is simplest; later filing needs statements or fees, and filing after allowance may require reopening (see MPEP 609 and the USPTO fee page).
- Disclosing a reference is not an admission that it is prior art or relevant.
- Breach can lead to unenforceability for inequitable conduct, which requires materiality and intent to deceive (Therasense).

Example (illustrative): your search log shows a 2019 paper close to claim 1. List it in an IDS with date and source; do not hide it because it hurts.

Disclose items from foreign counterparts' searches and related applications too.
