Double patenting stops one owner from getting a second patent on the same or an obvious variant of a claimed invention.

- **Statutory (same invention)**: claims identical in scope; cannot be fixed by disclaimer.
- **Obviousness-type (ODP)**: a judge-made rule: a later claim that is not patentably distinct from a claim in a commonly owned patent or application is rejected.

Fix: file a terminal disclaimer. The later patent then expires with the reference patent and stays enforceable only while commonly owned. A disclaimer is not an admission of obviousness.

Safe harbor: 35 USC 121 protects divisionals filed because of a restriction requirement, but only while they remain consonant with that restriction.

Term effects: In re Cellect (Fed. Cir. 2023) held ODP can invalidate a patent whose term was extended by patent term adjustment; later cases soften this for first-filed, first-issued patents. Check current law.

Example (illustrative): a continuation claims "checking a URL before launch with a local list"; the parent claims "checking before launch". Examiner finds them not distinct, so you file a terminal disclaimer to proceed.
