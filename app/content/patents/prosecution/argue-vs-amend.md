After a rejection you can traverse (argue), amend, or both.

| Situation | Usual move |
| Examiner overlooked a limitation | Argue, pointing to the claim language |
| Examiner read a term too broadly | Argue claim construction, support from the specification |
| Art genuinely close | Amend and argue the distinction |
| Rejection basis unclear | Interview, then clarify |

Costs to weigh:
- A narrowing amendment made for patentability presumptively surrenders equivalents in that range (see Festo estoppel).
- Arguments can also be used against you: statements distinguishing a reference may be treated as disclaimer or estoppel.
- Amendments need written-description support and cannot add new matter.
- Keep dependent claims as fallbacks and consider a continuation to preserve broader claims.

Example (illustrative): amending claim 1 to add "before launching the URL" distinguishes Smith. If the specification supports it, it is allowable, but later equivalents involving post-launch checks may be hard to assert.

Use measured, factual wording in responses.
