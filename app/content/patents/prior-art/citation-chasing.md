Once you find a close reference, follow its citation network.

- **Backward citations**: what the patent or paper cites, including references an examiner added. These show the older art the field relied on.
- **Forward citations**: later documents that cite it. These show newer work and who is active.
- **Family members**: same invention filed in other countries; their examiners may have cited different art. Use Global Dossier or Espacenet.
- **Prosecution history**: read the examiner's rejections and the applicant's arguments in Patent Center; they reveal what was considered close and how it was distinguished.

Example (illustrative): you find patent P about phishing checks. Backward citations lead to a 2015 security paper. Forward citations lead to a 2022 application claiming a pre-launch check, a closer hit.

Caution: a citation on the face of a patent is not a legal finding that it is relevant; read the cited document yourself. If you know of a material reference, disclosure obligations may apply (see IDS and duty of candor).

Log each reference you follow and why, so the trail is reproducible.

_Educational, not legal advice._
