Prior art is not only patents. Non-patent literature (NPL) includes journal and conference papers, preprints (arXiv), product manuals, standards documents, source-code repositories, web pages, talks, and products sold or publicly used.

What makes NPL count: it must have been publicly accessible before the effective filing date (a "printed publication" or otherwise available to the public) so that interested skilled people could find it with reasonable diligence. Under AIA 102(a)(1), public use and on-sale activity also count.

Practical points:
- Capture the publication date reliably: a version history, DOI record, or archive snapshot, not just a page footer.
- Software is often documented outside patents: release notes, API docs, commit history, Stack Overflow answers.
- Your own earlier publications can be prior art against you; AIA 102(b)(1) gives a one-year grace period in the US only, and most foreign systems give none.

Example (illustrative): a 2019 conference paper describes checking URLs before navigation. It may anticipate or be combined under 103 even though it is not a patent.

Cite NPL fully (author, title, venue, date, URL) in your log and in any disclosure to the USPTO.
