A claim is unpatentable if, at the effective filing date, the differences between it and the prior art would have been obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art (POSITA). Unlike 102, the examiner may combine several references.

Graham v. John Deere factors:
1. Scope and content of the prior art.
2. Differences between prior art and claim.
3. Level of ordinary skill.
4. Objective indicia (secondary considerations).

Under KSR v. Teleflex the analysis is flexible: a reason to combine can come from the references, market demand, design incentives, common sense, or "obvious to try" with a reasonable expectation of success. No rigid teaching-suggestion-motivation test is required.

Example (illustrative): Reference A scans and decodes QR codes. Reference B checks URLs for malware. Examiner argues a skilled person would add B's check to A. Responses: show a missing element, a teaching away, no reasonable expectation of success, a flawed rationale, or evidence of unexpected results or other objective indicia.

Hindsight is not allowed: the rationale must come from before the invention, not from the applicant's own claim.
