A search looks for disclosures that could anticipate or make your claims obvious, before and during drafting. It is never proof that nothing exists.

Workflow:
1. State the core inventive concept as functions, not product names.
2. List keywords, synonyms, and problem-oriented phrases ("reduce latency when checking links").
3. Run Boolean queries; read the claims and figures, not just abstracts.
4. Add classification searches (CPC).
5. Chase citations backward and forward.
6. Search non-patent literature.
7. Log everything.
8. Map hits to your claim elements.

Note: there is no general US duty to search, but anything you know that is material must be disclosed to the USPTO (see IDS).

Example (illustrative): for a QR URL checker, combine "QR code" with "uniform resource locator" and "malicious OR phishing OR reputation", then repeat in CPC G06F/H04L areas.

Search broadly across jurisdictions; foreign and non-English documents count as prior art too.
