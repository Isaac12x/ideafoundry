A search log is a dated record of what you searched, where, and what you found. It makes the search repeatable and feeds drafting and disclosure decisions.

Record per session:
- Date, database, and exact query string (with Boolean operators and CPC codes).
- Date range or filters used.
- Hits reviewed, with the document number and publication date.
- Relevance notes: which claim elements each hit covers.
- Dead ends and why, so you do not repeat them.
- Items to disclose to the USPTO.

Why it matters:
- Drafting: shows which features are old and which you can emphasize.
- Candor: if you know of a material reference, you may need to disclose it (see IDS); a log shows what you knew and when.
- Review: a second person can rerun it.

Note: US applicants generally have no duty to perform a search, but they do have a duty to disclose known material information. Keep the log factual; opinions about relevance written carelessly may be discoverable later.

Example (illustrative): "2026-11-20 | Patent Public Search | (QR NEAR3 code) AND (malicious OR phishing) AND URL | 42 hits | 3 close: US-X, US-Y, paper Z".
