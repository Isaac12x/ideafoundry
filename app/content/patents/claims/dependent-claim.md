A dependent claim refers back to an earlier claim and adds a further limitation ("The method of claim 1, wherein..."). It incorporates all the limits of the parent (35 USC 112(d)) and must narrow it.

Uses:
- **Fallback positions**: if claim 1 is rejected or invalidated, a narrower dependent may survive.
- **Specific embodiments**: preferred sizes, algorithms, data types.
- **Differentiating the product** from prior art or competitors' designs.

Test (MPEP 608.01(n)): a claim fails as dependent if it could be infringed without infringing the parent. It must also still be supported by the specification and have antecedent basis for "the" terms.

Example: Claim 2: "The method of claim 1, wherein the reputation database is stored on the mobile device." Claim 3: "The method of claim 2, wherein the database is updated daily."

Tip: dependents should add real technical detail, not bare advantage statements ("wherein performance is improved"). Each should be one more fence a competitor must go around.
