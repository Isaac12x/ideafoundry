A claim is more likely eligible when it improves the functioning of a computer or other technology rather than just using a computer as a tool for an abstract idea.

Cases:
- **Enfish v. Microsoft** (2016): a self-referential database table was an improvement in how computers store data.
- **McRO v. Bandai** (2016): specific rules automating lip-synch animation were a technological improvement.
- **Electric Power Group** (2016): collecting, analyzing, and displaying information, without a technological improvement, was abstract.
- **Recentive v. Fox** (Fed. Cir. 2025): applying generic machine-learning methods to a new field was not enough.

At the USPTO this is Step 2A prong 2: does the claim integrate the exception into a practical application? MPEP 2106.04(d) gives considerations.

Drafting advice:
- State the technical problem, then the specific solution in the claim, not only the result.
- Explain the improvement (speed, memory, accuracy, security) in the specification.
- Avoid claims that only recite results or generic components.

Example: "reducing lookup memory by storing a bloom filter and verifying only on a positive" ties the claim to a specific, explained improvement. Support improvement statements with real data or logic, not invented numbers.
