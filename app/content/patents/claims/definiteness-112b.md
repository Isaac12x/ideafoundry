35 USC 112(b) requires claims to "particularly point out and distinctly claim" the invention. In Nautilus v. Biosig (2014) the Supreme Court held a claim is indefinite if, read in light of the specification and prosecution history, it fails to inform a skilled person of the scope with **reasonable certainty**. (This replaced the older "insolubly ambiguous" test.)

Common problems:
- Unquantified relative terms ("fast", "large", "about", "substantially") with no standard in the specification.
- Missing antecedent basis.
- Mixing categories in one claim (method plus apparatus).
- Functional language with no structure (see 112(f)).
- "At least one of A, B, and C" style lists with ambiguous meaning.

Example: "A fast verification" is vague; "a verification completing within a time threshold stored in a configuration parameter" is anchored.

During examination the USPTO applies a lower threshold than courts: a claim that is merely unclear can be rejected, and the fix is amendment. Terms of degree can pass if the specification supplies objective boundaries (MPEP 2173.05(b)).
