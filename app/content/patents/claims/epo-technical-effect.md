The EPO does not use the US "abstract idea" test. Under Articles 52(2) and (3) EPC, programs for computers "as such" are excluded, but an invention with technical character is not. The approach (the "COMVIK" approach, T 641/00):

1. Identify the claim features that contribute to the technical character, those that bring about a technical effect or serve a technical purpose.
2. Non-technical features (business rules, presentation of information, mere algorithms) are ignored when assessing inventive step, unless they interact with the technical means to produce a technical effect.
3. The objective technical problem is formulated from the technical features and effect.

Example: a QR verification claim with only "a business rule" lists lacks technical contribution; one that reduces network traffic by a specific caching scheme does have a technical effect.

Compared with the US: both look for a technical improvement; the EPO assesses it at novelty and inventive step, and the US at eligibility first.

Jurisdiction note: this applies to EP filings (and EP phase of PCT). See the EPO Guidelines for current wording.
