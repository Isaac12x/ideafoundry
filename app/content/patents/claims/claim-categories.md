US law (35 USC 101) allows four statutory categories: process, machine, manufacture, and composition of matter. In drafting these usually appear as:

- **Apparatus / device**: a physical thing with parts.
- **Method / process**: ordered steps.
- **System**: components that work together, often hardware plus software.
- **Composition**: materials or mixtures.
- **Computer-readable medium (Beauregard)**: a storage medium holding instructions.

Why several: each category is infringed by different actors. A method claim is infringed by whoever performs the steps (often a user); a system or medium claim is infringed by whoever makes or sells it. Software inventions are commonly drafted in method, system, and medium forms so each variant is covered.

Example: one QR-verification invention claimed as (1) a method of verifying, (2) a mobile device configured to verify, (3) a non-transitory medium storing verifying instructions.

Each category still has to pass the eligibility test (see Patent eligibility).
