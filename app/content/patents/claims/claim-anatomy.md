Every claim has three parts in one sentence:

1. **Preamble**: names the claimed subject ("A method of verifying a URL"). It may or may not limit the claim; courts ask whether it gives life and meaning to the body or states a necessary structure.
2. **Transition**: the joining word(s) such as "comprising" that fix how open or closed the claim is. See Transitional phrases.
3. **Body**: the elements and how they relate, each step or part separated by semicolons or line breaks.

Example:
"A system for verifying a link, **comprising**: a processor; and a memory storing instructions that, when executed, cause the processor to compare a decoded URL against a stored list."
Preamble: "A system for verifying a link". Transition: "comprising". Body: the processor and memory elements.

Tips: introduce each element with "a/an" the first time and "the" afterward (antecedent basis); keep one claim to one sentence; state relationships between elements, not just a list of parts.
