35 USC 112(f) lets an element be claimed as a "means or step for performing a specified function" without reciting structure, but the claim then covers only the **structure, material, or acts disclosed in the specification** and their equivalents.

Triggers: "means for ..." (presumed), and nonce words with no structural meaning such as "module", "unit", "mechanism", "element for" followed by a function (Williamson v. Citrix, Fed. Cir. 2015, en banc).

Computer-implemented functions: the specification must disclose an **algorithm** (steps, flowchart, formula) for the function, not just a general-purpose computer, or the claim is indefinite (Aristocrat; WMS Gaming).

Example: "means for verifying a URL" needs a disclosed verification algorithm in the specification. "A processor configured to compare the URL to a list" recites a structure and typically avoids 112(f).

Tip: either use 112(f) deliberately and write the algorithm into the specification, or avoid it by claiming structure plus operation. An accidental 112(f) reading narrows the claim and can make it invalid.
