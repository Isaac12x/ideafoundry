A Beauregard claim (named for In re Beauregard) claims an article of manufacture: a computer-readable storage medium holding instructions that, when executed by a processor, perform steps. It lets the patentee reach software sold or distributed on media or as a download.

Eligibility caution: a bare "computer program" or a medium that could include signals or carrier waves is not a statutory category. USPTO practice (and cases such as In re Nuijten) therefore calls for "non-transitory" wording. Even then, the claim must still survive the two-step abstract-idea analysis; putting an abstract method on a medium does not rescue it.

Example:
"A non-transitory computer-readable medium storing instructions that, when executed by one or more processors, cause the processors to: decode an encoded identifier from an image; compare a hostname of the identifier to a stored list; and block navigation when no match is found."

Tip: mirror the method claim's steps, so a single support passage in the specification covers all three forms (method, system, medium).
