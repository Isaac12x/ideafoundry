An independent claim stands alone and does not refer to any other claim. It states the complete combination of elements for one invention and in one category. Claim 1 is usually independent and the broadest.

Why it matters:
- Its scope is set by the fewest elements needed for novelty and non-obviousness; every added element narrows it.
- Competitors can only avoid it by removing or changing an element.
- Claim fees depend on counts: the USPTO base fee covers up to three independent and twenty total claims; extra ones cost more (see the fee schedule on uspto.gov).

Example: "A method of verifying a link, comprising: decoding a code to a URL; querying a reputation database with the URL; and displaying a result." Claim 1 is independent; "The method of claim 1, wherein the database is local" is dependent.

Tip: draft the independent claim from the inventive concept, then test it against 101 (eligibility) and the prior art. Broadest-possible is a goal bounded by support (112(a)), definiteness, and eligibility, not "as abstract as you can".
