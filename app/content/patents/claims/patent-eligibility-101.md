35 USC 101 limits patents to a process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter. Judicial exceptions (abstract ideas, laws of nature, natural phenomena) are not patentable by themselves.

USPTO analysis (MPEP 2106, 2019 revised guidance):
1. Step 1: is the claim one of the four categories?
2. Step 2A prong 1: does it recite an exception? For abstract ideas the groupings are mathematical concepts, certain methods of organizing human activity, and mental processes.
3. Step 2A prong 2: does the claim integrate the exception into a practical application (such as a technical improvement)?
4. Step 2B: does it add significantly more (an inventive concept beyond well-understood, routine, conventional activity)? Whether something is well-understood and conventional is a fact question (Berkheimer v. HP).

Court cases: Alice/Mayo; Enfish and McRO (specific technical improvements eligible); Electric Power Group (collecting, analyzing, displaying data is abstract); Recentive v. Fox (2025) (applying generic machine learning to a new data environment is not enough).

Source rules of thumb ("pen and paper" test, "remove the computer") are heuristics only, not the legal test.

Example: a claim that only "decodes a QR code and checks a URL list" is at risk; add a specific technical mechanism and describe the improvement.
