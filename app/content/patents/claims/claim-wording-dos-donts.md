Do:
- Use "comprising" and a clear preamble.
- Introduce each element with "a/an", refer back with "the".
- Define terms of degree with objective anchors found in the specification.
- Claim *how* (steps, structure) rather than only the result.
- Keep one idea per element; use semicolons.
- Draft in several categories (method, system, medium) from the same support.
- Draft dependents that add real technical detail.

Don't:
- Use unquantified relative terms ("fast", "large", "substantially" without a standard).
- Write "means for" by accident (see 112(f)).
- Use "wherein" for pure advantage statements; they add little weight.
- Add negative limitations ("not X") without specification support.
- Use "essentially" or "approximately" casually.
- Put non-technical or purely human-activity steps in the core.

Example: Replace "quickly determining a risk" with "determining a risk score within a latency threshold stored in the configuration".

Tip: read the claims aloud as one sentence, then check every term against the specification.
